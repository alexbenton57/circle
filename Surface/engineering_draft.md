# Circle Repo/ Surface Branch

## Project Context

My friend, (Matt,) and I (Alex) are planning to enter the Guthman Musical Instrument Competition with an evolution of the Circle Gesture System (GCS.) His role will be mostly software - to develop the CGL and synth engine, predominatly made in max MSP. My role is to build the physical instrument.

Currently, the GCS consists of a MIDI keyboard, an iPad touch interface, a couple of pedals plugged into the keyboard, and a laptop running Max. 

The eventual goal is a box with a MIDI keyboard, a rasp pi running max, and a novel mechanical XY input device. The mechanical XY input is likely to consist of 3 spools with return springs and a damping feature, such as a magnetic brake. The XY position of the stylus connected to the 3 spools will be triangulated on an MCU using data from rotary encoders on each spool, and sent to the pi over serial. The purpose of this mechanical input with force feedback is so that the musician must put in effort to make a sound - a rare feature of digital instruments. We also want to avoid using a touchscreen as kaoss pads have been done before.

There may also be a visual indicator of the two-ellipse curve generated by the XY input, possibly projected using an XY galvanometer, driven by a seperate MCU and communicating over serial. A small touch screen and some knobs and dials may also be present for additional IO. 

That eventual goal is far away. Right now I need an intermediate development stage so I can test out the force feedback and physical feeling of the instrument. Initially I want to replace the rotary encoders, galvanometer, MIDI keyboard, pi, and pedals with a Microsoft Surface Pro 4 running max and a custom pyside6 app presenting the user with the necessary controls. The Surface will be placed in a mechanical rig with the spools and magnetic brakes so I can independently test the feeling of the instrument.

See the README.md in repo root for information on the existing CGS and CGL.

## Branch Goals ('Surface')

This branch is for implementing that Surface-based intermediate step. Development may either be done on my main laptop or on the surface itself. The surface app must:

- Use the existing Max patch with minimal changes (ideally zero,) relying on existing max interfaces.
- Play the sounds coming from the max patch
- Present the user with a pyside 6 app which talks to the max patch. The pyside 6 app must contain:
    - An onscreen midi keyboard emulator.
    - An area of the screen for drawing circles
    - Readouts of the CGL parameters derived from the drawn circles
    - At a later date, a display of the two-ellipse curve defined by the CGL parameters on the circle drawing area
